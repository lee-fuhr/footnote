The idea

There’s a reason Rousseau walked. And Jobs, and Darwin. Movement does something to thought, loosens it, sends it somewhere the desk never could.

Walk-ideas are a specific kind of thing. Sparkling, right-brained, half-formed. They arrive connected and urgent, and then the moment you stop moving, they scatter. You get home and the thread is gone. What you actually need isn’t just to take a note. It’s a way to hold on to what came to you out there, and do something with it.

Footnote is that. Minimal enough that opening it doesn’t interrupt the walk. Structured enough to make sense of it after.

How it works

01

Start a walk

Open the app and begin. No setup, no account, nothing to configure. It’s ready when you are.

02

Type or talk

When something comes to you, tap and write. One sentence or several, whatever the moment asks for.

03

It’s saved with the moment

Every note records the exact time and your location. Later, you can read it back as a journal of the walk.

The science

Researchers at Stanford found that walking increased creative output in 81% of participants. The effect held even after sitting back down. Walking activates divergent thinking, the kind that generates novel connections, not just efficient ones.

Darwin walked the same gravel loop at Down House every day for decades, counting laps with a pile of flints. Rousseau wrote: “My mind only works with my legs.” Steve Jobs held every serious conversation on foot. These aren’t coincidences.

Exercise increases BDNF, the protein that supports new neural connections. Rhythmic movement frees the prefrontal cortex. A walk is not a break from thinking. It’s a different kind of thinking.

Oppezzo & Schwartz, 2014: walking and creative thinking (Stanford) Cotman & Berchtold, 2002: exercise and BDNF brain plasticity Aga et al., 2021: aerobic exercise and divergent thinking

Privacy

Your walks and their location stay on your device. No account, no login, no name, no profile of you. The only thing that ever leaves is your walk text, sent to Claude when you turn on AI analysis, and discarded right after.

Footnote is bound to your device, not a sign-in. Walks back up to your own iCloud Drive so you don’t lose them, and that copy is yours. GPS is recorded with each note only to show you where a thought arrived. With AI analysis off, your walks and location never leave your device. (Voting on what to build next is the one small exception, and it sends only your choice, never your walks.)
